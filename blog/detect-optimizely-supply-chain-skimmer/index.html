Detecting the Optimizely Skimmer: Blocked or Reached, Embedded or Fetched, and the Rules That Tell Them Apart

The Optimizely Web compromise ran a wallet clipper and card skimmer in visitors' browsers from a domain everyone trusts. I detonated the real payload in a sealed lab, then wrote detections for both sides. For your users: DNS, proxy and EDR rules that separate a blocked request from a real exposure. For your own sites: snippet integrity, CSP-report and synthetic-browser checks. Every rule is given for Splunk + CrowdStrike and Sentinel + Defender for Endpoint.

Key Sections: What Happened · The Attack, Step by Step · Pyramid of Pain · Network Detections · Endpoint Detections · Web Detections (Your Own Sites) · When It Fires · Hardening · IOCs

Part of my detection series, and the companion to The Optimizely Skimmer, Detonated. Org A runs Splunk with CrowdStrike Falcon and Cisco Umbrella. Org B runs Microsoft Sentinel with Defender for Endpoint. The rules are built from what my lab recorded (DNS, proxy, browser and exfiltration traffic from the real final build). I haven't run them against production EDR data, so check field names and log sources in your environment.

1. What Happened

On 16 November 2026 an attacker added a loader to the Project JavaScript of Optimizely Web customers. For about 16 hours, visitors to 178 organisations' sites downloaded track.js from 1688360[.]cc, through a trusted cdn.optimizely.com file. The payload:

  • swapped crypto addresses on the page, in the clipboard and in form fields (8 chains, checksum-validated);
  • sent card number, expiry and CVV to card.php with sendBeacon;
  • sent the URL, readable cookies and an html2canvas screenshot to hit.php;
  • sent older iPhones to .icu routers and a hidden iframe leading to the Coruna and DarkSword kits.

Two different teams care about this. The SOC wants to know which users were exposed. Web and AppSec teams want to know whether their own site served it. This post covers both.

2. The Attack, Step by Step

Initial Access Step 1: Write access to a SaaS tag tool

The attacker edited Optimizely Project JavaScript in many customer projects (T1195.002 Compromise Software Supply Chain). How they got access is unconfirmed. The CDN then served the edited file to every visitor (T1189 Drive-by Compromise).

Execution Step 2: One of three loaders

The loader is a plain <script src>, an atob()-hidden URL, or a Base64 copy of the whole payload run with (0,eval) when the remote load fails (T1059.007, T1027). The third one never touches the IOC domain unless it exfiltrates.

Collection Step 3: Clip, skim, screenshot

Address replacement in the page and clipboard (T1565.003 Runtime Data Manipulation, T1115 Clipboard Data), card capture from form and fetch/XHR bodies (T1056.003 Web Portal Capture), cookies (T1539) and a screenshot (T1113).

Exfiltration Step 4: Beacon home

POST /hit.php (text/plain JSON, about 25 KB with the screenshot) and a sendBeacon to /card.php (about 73 bytes) on the payload host (T1041).

Exploitation Step 5: iOS only

iPhones and iPads load boot.js from two .icu routers. On iOS 18.6 and earlier, a 0x0 iframe loads /JIUJIU888/weifile.html and then the exploit kits (T1203). Pages reload to retry.

4. Network Detections

N1. Every IOC contact, split into blocked and reached (T1189)

This is the triage rule. Umbrella answers blocked lookups with an address in 146.112.0.0/16. Defender then logs ConnectionSuccess to that address, because the browser did reach the block page. That's a block, not a hit. In my lab's block mode the browser got the block page and the payload never loaded.

Org A

(index=umbrella sourcetype="cisco:umbrella:dns") OR (index=crowdstrike event_simpleName=DnsRequest)
    (query="*1688360.cc*" OR query="*webshell.lol*" OR query="*5vobauua3ojpklx.icu*" OR query="*eil786npto9u5bq.icu*"
     OR query="*ua23.online*" OR DomainName="*1688360.cc" OR DomainName="*webshell.lol"
     OR DomainName="5vobauua3ojpklx.icu" OR DomainName="eil786npto9u5bq.icu" OR DomainName="ua23.online")
| eval domain=coalesce(query, DomainName), host=coalesce(identities, ComputerName)
| eval verdict=case(match(action,"(?i)block"), "blocked (Umbrella)",
                    match(IP4Records,"^146\.112\."), "blocked (block-page IP)",
                    true(), "REACHED - check")
| stats count min(_time) as first max(_time) as last values(verdict) as verdict by host domain
| convert ctime(first) ctime(last)

Org B

let IOCs = dynamic(["1688360.cc","webshell.lol","5vobauua3ojpklx.icu","eil786npto9u5bq.icu","ua23.online"]);
DeviceNetworkEvents
| where Timestamp between (datetime(2026-11-09) .. now())
| where RemoteUrl has_any (IOCs)
| extend Verdict = iff(ipv4_is_in_range(RemoteIP, "146.112.0.0/16"), "Blocked (block-page IP)", "REACHED - check")
| summarize Events = count(), First = min(Timestamp), Last = max(Timestamp), RemoteIPs = make_set(RemoteIP, 5),
            Browsers = make_set(InitiatingProcessFileName, 5) by DeviceName, RemoteUrl, ActionType, Verdict
| order by Verdict desc, Events desc

N2. Optimizely, then the IOC, within two minutes

This is the supply-chain shape: a trusted snippet loads, and a domain nobody has seen before follows immediately. The IOC version is below. For the generic version, replace has_any (IOCs) with "domain first seen in the last 7 days".

Org A

index=crowdstrike event_simpleName=DnsRequest
    (DomainName="cdn.optimizely.com" OR DomainName="*1688360.cc" OR DomainName="*webshell.lol" OR DomainName="*.icu")
| eval kind=if(DomainName="cdn.optimizely.com","opt","ioc")
| sort 0 aid ContextProcessId _time
| streamstats current=f last(_time) as prev_t last(kind) as prev_kind by aid ContextProcessId
| where kind="ioc" AND prev_kind="opt" AND (_time-prev_t)<=120
| table _time ComputerName DomainName ContextProcessId

Org B

let IOCs = dynamic(["1688360.cc","webshell.lol","5vobauua3ojpklx.icu","eil786npto9u5bq.icu","ua23.online"]);
let Opt = DeviceNetworkEvents
| where RemoteUrl has "cdn.optimizely.com"
| project DeviceId, OptTime = Timestamp, InitiatingProcessId;
DeviceNetworkEvents
| where RemoteUrl has_any (IOCs)
| join kind=inner Opt on DeviceId, InitiatingProcessId
| where Timestamp between (OptTime .. (OptTime + 2m))
| summarize OptimizelyAt = max(OptTime) by DeviceName, Timestamp, RemoteUrl, RemoteIP, InitiatingProcessFileName

N3. The exfiltration shape on the proxy, whatever the domain

In the lab every exfiltration was a cross-site POST from a browser to /hit.php, /card.php or /hook.php. hit.php was text/plain of about 25 KB; card.php was 73 bytes, form-encoded. Domains change, but the operator kept these paths all day.

Org A

| tstats count values(Web.http_referrer) as referrer values(Web.bytes_out) as bytes_out from datamodel=Web
    where Web.http_method=POST (Web.uri_path="/hit.php" OR Web.uri_path="/card.php" OR Web.uri_path="/hook.php")
    by Web.src Web.dest Web.uri_path
| where mvcount(referrer) > 0

Org B (ASIM web session, so any proxy or SWG connector works)

_Im_WebSession(starttime = ago(14d))
| where HttpRequestMethod == "POST"
| extend Path = tostring(parse_url(Url).Path)
| where Path in ("/hit.php", "/card.php", "/hook.php")
| where isnotempty(HttpReferrer) and parse_url(HttpReferrer).Host != parse_url(Url).Host
| summarize Posts = count(), Bytes = sum(SrcBytes), Sites = make_set(tostring(parse_url(HttpReferrer).Host), 10)
            by SrcIpAddr, DstHostname = tostring(parse_url(Url).Host), Path

N4. A phone walking the iOS delivery chain (T1203)

MDE won't see an iPhone, but your proxy, SWG or mobile DNS service will. The chain is distinctive: boot.js on a random .icu name, then /JIUJIU888/, /A8/, /B9/, /C10/, /D11/ or /D12/.

Org A

| tstats count values(Web.url) as urls from datamodel=Web
    where (Web.url="*.icu/boot.js*" OR Web.url="*/JIUJIU888/*" OR Web.url="*.icu/A8/*" OR Web.url="*.icu/B9/*"
           OR Web.url="*.icu/C10/*" OR Web.url="*.icu/D11/*" OR Web.url="*.icu/D12/*")
    by Web.src Web.http_user_agent
| search Web.http_user_agent="*iPhone*" OR Web.http_user_agent="*iPad*"

Org B

_Im_WebSession(starttime = ago(14d))
| where Url matches regex @"\.icu/(boot\.js|JIUJIU888/|A8/|B9/|C10/|D11/|D12/)"
| where HttpUserAgent has_any ("iPhone", "iPad")
| summarize Hits = count(), Paths = make_set(tostring(parse_url(Url).Path), 10), First = min(TimeGenerated)
            by SrcIpAddr, SrcUsername, HttpUserAgent

Any result here is an Escalate. The device should be checked for its iOS version (anything below 18.7.3 or 26.3 was in scope) and handled as possibly compromised.

5. Endpoint Detections

E1. A browser reached the real infrastructure and sent data

The opposite of a block: the browser connected to the IOC on a non-block address and sent more than a handshake. In the lab, hit.php uploads were about 25 KB.

Org A

index=crowdstrike event_simpleName=NetworkConnectIP4
    RemoteAddressIP4 IN ("136.113.106[.]136","34.96.213[.]201","34.87.36[.]58")
    ContextBaseFileName IN ("msedge.exe","chrome.exe","firefox.exe","brave.exe")
| stats count min(_time) as first by ComputerName UserName ContextBaseFileName RemoteAddressIP4 RemotePort

Org B

let IOCs = dynamic(["1688360.cc","webshell.lol","5vobauua3ojpklx.icu","eil786npto9u5bq.icu","ua23.online"]);
let IOCIPs = dynamic(["136.113.106[.]136","34.96.213[.]201","34.87.36[.]58"]);
DeviceNetworkEvents
| where (RemoteUrl has_any (IOCs) or RemoteIP in (IOCIPs)) and ActionType == "ConnectionSuccess"
| where not(ipv4_is_in_range(RemoteIP, "146.112.0.0/16"))
| project Timestamp, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessAccountName, InitiatingProcessFileName, RemoteUrl, RemoteIP, RemotePort

MDE doesn't record per-connection byte counts in this table, so pair a hit with your proxy's bytes-out for the same minute (N3).

E2. The skimmer's own breadcrumb in the browser profile

To avoid sending a card twice, the skimmer writes cc_<number><expiry><cvv> to sessionStorage in plain text. Chromium persists session storage to the profile's Session Storage LevelDB folder, so on an E1 host you may be able to confirm a capture without asking the user. Run it through Falcon RTR or MDE Live Response as a script. It's a forensic check, not a SIEM rule. I haven't validated it in the lab, because Playwright throws its browser profiles away, so test it on a known-good browser first. Chromium can store the string as one-byte or UTF-16 text, so the script checks both:

# PowerShell: look for the skimmer's dedup key in Edge/Chrome Session Storage (ASCII or UTF-16)
$roots = "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Microsoft\Edge\User Data", "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Google\Chrome\User Data"
$rx = 'cc_\d{13,19}\d{2}/\d{2}\d{3,4}'
Get-ChildItem $roots -Recurse -Include *.log,*.ldb -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue |
  Where-Object { $_.DirectoryName -like '*Session Storage*' } | ForEach-Object {
    $b = [IO.File]::ReadAllBytes($_.FullName)
    if ([Text.Encoding]::ASCII.GetString($b) -match $rx -or [Text.Encoding]::Unicode.GetString($b) -match $rx) {
      "$($_.FullName)  (match: card captured; don't paste the value into a ticket)" } }

A match means a card was captured in that browser. Whether it reached the attacker depends on E1 and N3. Treat it as exposed either way.

6. Web Detections (Your Own Sites)

W1. Your vendor snippet changed, and the change looks like a loader

No SIEM sees inside a third-party file, so fetch it yourself. A scheduled job pulls every third-party script your pages load (Optimizely, tag managers, chat widgets) every five minutes and logs a hash, the domains inside it and loader tokens. Alert on a change that adds a domain, atob(, (0,eval)( or new Function(. On 16 November that would have fired within minutes of the first infected write (03:55 UTC on the BBC snippet, per HECAVEX), hours before the first public sighting.

Org A (custom sourcetype vendor:snippet)

index=appsec sourcetype=vendor:snippet
| sort 0 url _time
| streamstats current=f last(sha256) as prev_sha last(domains) as prev_domains by url
| where sha256!=prev_sha
| eval new_domains=mvmap(domains, if(isnull(mvfind(prev_domains, "^".replace(domains,"\.","\\.")."$")), domains, null()))
| where isnotnull(new_domains) OR match(loader_tokens,"atob|eval|Function")
| table _time url prev_sha sha256 new_domains loader_tokens

Org B (custom table VendorSnippet_CL)

VendorSnippet_CL
| order by Url asc, TimeGenerated asc
| extend PrevSha = prev(Sha256, 1), PrevDomains = prev(Domains, 1), SameUrl = Url == prev(Url, 1)
| where SameUrl and Sha256 != PrevSha
| extend NewDomains = set_difference(todynamic(Domains), todynamic(PrevDomains))
| where array_length(NewDomains) > 0 or LoaderTokens has_any ("atob", "eval", "Function")
| project TimeGenerated, Url, PrevSha, Sha256, NewDomains, LoaderTokens

W2. CSP reports naming a script-src or connect-src you've never allowed

In the lab the CSP-protected page produced four violation reports per view: track.js and html2canvas (script-src-elem), then hit.php and card.php (connect-src). If you collect CSP reports, a new blocked host showing up across many users at once is a supply-chain signal.

Org A

index=appsec sourcetype=csp:report
| rename "csp-report.blocked-uri" as blocked_uri, "csp-report.effective-directive" as directive, "csp-report.source-file" as source_file
| eval blocked_host=replace(blocked_uri, "^https?://([^/:]+).*", "\1")
| stats dc(src) as users values(directive) as directives values(source_file) as source_files earliest(_time) as first by blocked_host
| where users >= 5 AND first >= relative_time(now(), "-1d")

Org B

CspReport_CL
| extend BlockedHost = tostring(parse_url(BlockedUri).Host)
| summarize Users = dcount(ClientIp), Directives = make_set(EffectiveDirective), SourceFiles = make_set(SourceFile, 5),
            First = min(TimeGenerated) by BlockedHost
| where Users >= 5 and First > ago(1d)

Remember what the lab showed: CSP stopped the exfiltration, but the clipper still worked. A W2 alert means the code ran on your page, not only that it was blocked.

W3. A synthetic browser that checks for the payload's own markers

Point a headless browser at your key pages every few minutes and evaluate one line. Every build since 15:21 sets the stray global TASK, and all of them set _k7. Patched built-ins are a generic sign of a hooking skimmer:

({ k7: !!window._k7, cc: !!window._cc, task: window.TASK === 1,
   callHooked: Function.prototype.call.toString().indexOf('[native code]') < 0,
   fetchHooked: window.fetch.toString().indexOf('[native code]') < 0 })

Send the result to your SIEM and alert on any true. The two "hooked" checks will need an allowlist for legitimate tools that wrap fetch (some RUM agents do), but nothing legitimate should patch Function.prototype.call.

8. Hardening

  • Treat tag-manager and A/B-tool accounts as production deploy rights: SSO, MFA, least privilege, audit-log export to the SIEM and an approval step for custom code.
  • Remove 'unsafe-eval' from script-src where you can. In the lab it was the only thing that let the embedded copy run.
  • Set a tight connect-src and collect reports (W2). It blocked every exfiltration in the lab.
  • Keep card entry in the payment provider's cross-origin iframe. The skimmer couldn't read across origins.
  • Block newly registered domains at the DNS filter. Every domain here was days old.
  • Keep managed iPhones on current iOS, and route mobile traffic through a DNS filter so N4 has data.
